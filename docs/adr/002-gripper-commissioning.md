# ADR 002 — Franka gripper commissioning

On 18 November 2026 the first complete runs with a 20 N drive-force limit timed out while grasping the 50 g part. Cell A entered the abort sequence at step 2,068 and Cell B at step 1,648. Both reached the 7,200-step horizon with a failed goal. The Windows and WSL2 replays agree on the requirement verdicts and first witnesses for each run. [Cell A](../../results/workstation/commissioning-a-before-tuning-wsl.json) · [Cell B](../../results/workstation/commissioning-b-before-tuning-wsl.json)

We tested a 4 N upper limit per independently actuated finger and made an explicit hold command begin from the measured joint position. The passing Cell A diagnostic completed its transfer at step 4,080 and reached the horizon with task SUCCESS and compliance PASS. The corresponding Cell B diagnostic still timed out. These were single commissioning runs. The nominal repeated-run evidence is collected separately under `commission-v1`. [Cell A Windows replay](../../results/workstation/commissioning-a-4n-windows.json) · [Cell A WSL2 replay](../../results/workstation/commissioning-a-4n-wsl.json)

The change affects the contact dynamics and simulated time to complete a grasp. The position, velocity and dwell requirements used to recognize a completed grasp apply to every diagnostic. The simulated steps, goal deadline and evaluation seeds continue to follow the versioned experiment protocols.

Further commissioning compared effective finger-joint inertia with the same 4 N cap, a 0.022 m/s finger slew limit, 4,000 N/m stiffness and 80 N·s/m damping. The solver used CPU TGS, one articulation velocity iteration and articulation contacts last. At 0.1 kg per prismatic finger joint, Cell B completed one boundary state. On pilot seed 0 the fingers held the part after lift, but the controller timed out waiting for the gripper signal before carrying it. At 10 kg of reflected inertia per finger joint, the same pilot state passed the physical placement criterion at step 6,641. The controller had yet to declare DONE at the 7,200-step horizon. The recorded task outcome was SUCCESS and compliance was PASS. These values describe simulated actuator tuning. They do not measure the inertia of real Franka fingers. [Windows replay](../../results/workstation/commissioning-b-10kg-windows.json) · [WSL2 replay](../../results/workstation/commissioning-b-10kg-wsl.json)

With a 0.040 m/s opening slew and the same 0.022 m/s closing slew, Cell B reached the physical goal at step 6,430 on pilot seed 0 and declared DONE at 7,044. Separate opening and closing limits reproduced the measured closed-position stability and allowed the two release motions to finish sooner. Both replays agreed on every verdict and first witness. The original quickstart selected scene seed 1000. Developers saw the outcomes for this initial state while tuning both cells. It is part of the registered 1000–1019 block and must be identified in any analysis of performance on previously unseen states. ## Loaded ascent at Cell A boundary 3

On 18 November 2026, the first qualification attempt at Cell A corner 3 (−1 mm in X, +4 mm in Y, +0.03 rad yaw) reached its destination but failed the carry invariant R09. With a 0.10 m/s loaded target speed cap, both fingers contacted the cube at step 1,922. The oracle recorded the vector from TCP to part at that step. The lift began at step 1,984 with a measured gap of 40.53 mm. R09 began checking 24 steps later. At step 2,043 the TCP was rising at 0.082 m/s while the part was falling at 0.077 m/s. The two fingers still contacted the part. The relative displacement exceeded 18 mm for 1,214 consecutive samples and peaked at 22.891 mm. This attempt stopped the original boundary qualification. [Paired WSL2 replay](../../results/workstation/loaded-ascent-a3.json)

A development run at the same observed corner limited target speed to 0.04 m/s for the first 30 mm of loaded ascent, then allowed 0.10 m/s. Both runs had the same recorded grasp vector, lift-admission state, measured 4 N drive limit and 18 mm R09 limit. Peak drift fell to 7.493 mm. The physical goal moved from step 3,500 to 3,558 and controller DONE from 4,104 to 4,164. At the 7,200-step horizon the WSL2 replay agreed with Windows on every requirement and first witness in both runs. [Measurements and source hashes](../../results/workstation/loaded-ascent-a3.json)

The A3 comparison covers one previously observed corner. The proposed common motion profile also applies to Cell B's upward moves from the source and from the fixture after processing. Acceptance of the common profile depends on both lifts, the final goal and controller completion within the registered horizon, followed by qualification on the registered cases. ## Interpreting the canonical results

The primary analysis includes all 820 registered rows. The secondary analysis groups the same rows by exposure during development. Seed 1000 accounts for 68 planned rows (34 per cell). Seeds 1001–1019, unseen during development, account for 752 planned rows (376 per cell). The first group contains 32 reference-controller rows and 36 mutant-controller rows. The second contains 608 reference rows and 144 mutant rows. Mutants cover only seeds 1000–1004. Differences between the pooled rates can therefore reflect the mix of controllers and fault arms. [Registered design](../../protocols/canonical-v1.yaml)

In each cell, every controller, fault arm and repeat for a seed reuses the same initial physical state. Seed 1000 represents one such state per cell. The other 19 seeds represent 19 states per cell. The secondary tables describe these repeated observations and give counts for matching cell, controller and arm. They report first-attempt allocations, starts, evaluable outcomes and task successes. A planned run that has yet to start stays in the planned denominator. A later retry stays in the retry ledger.

The generated Markdown and HTML reports show both cohorts by cell. The `seed_sensitivity` section of `metrics.json` and `tables/seed_sensitivity.json` contain the plan and protocol hashes, individual run IDs, repeat indices, initial-state hashes, first-attempt IDs, and available artifact digests. These data permit the reported counts to be traced back to the registered rows and the independently replayed attempts.
